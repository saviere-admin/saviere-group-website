Corporate accountability

Growth without shortcuts.

Governance begins with the quality of everyday decisions. Savière’s public commitments should be clear, proportionate to evidence and honest about the difference between policy, work in progress and proven practice.

01

Evidence before assertion

Product, market, performance and impact claims should be supported by appropriate evidence. Where evidence is incomplete, the uncertainty should be stated rather than hidden.

02

Clarity about operating status

Public communication should distinguish active work from plans and ambitions. Locations, partners, facilities, customers and capabilities should not be presented as established unless that is factually accurate.

03

Financial and operational discipline

Decisions should account for unit economics, reliability, customer experience, suppliers, compliance and the operational requirements of sustaining a business—not only its growth narrative.

04

Responsible communication

Announcements should be useful and verifiable. Media coverage must be genuine third-party coverage, and company milestones should be documented on Savière’s own website where appropriate.

05

Continuous improvement

Governance systems should mature alongside the business. This page sets out principles; it does not claim that every future audit, committee, control or process has already been implemented.

A living standard

Trust is built by matching words to actions.

As the company develops, this page should be updated with specific, approved information about implemented policies, responsible officers and formal governance arrangements. Until then, it intentionally describes principles rather than claiming the existence of unverified structures.
